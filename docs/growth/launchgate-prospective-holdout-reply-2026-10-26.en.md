We froze the product and protocol before generating 24 new numeric/boolean fixtures, with shuffled rules and records, four trap types, and min/max ranking. This is a prospective generated set, not independently authored or blinded.

It produced natural failures this time: 89/96 first proposals were valid. All seven errors chose a suboptimal admissible record while retaining the correct eligible set. Five were repaired and two withheld. Clean controls finished 48/48; injected cases finished 46/48. Withholding is not counted as task success.

Separately, controlled invalid seeds finished 45/48, with three withheld. Across both live populations, seven trajectories reached the second correction: two then passed and five were withheld. All delivered frames passed the independent arithmetic check; notices were present throughout. The separate deterministic controls also passed, including incomplete/no-eligible refusals.

The remaining weakness was ranking feedback: `not-optimal` named the problem without showing the comparison. After recording the frozen results, I added a safe numeric witness—for example, selected 87 versus eligible best 84 under `min`—and bounded missing-set evidence. Linux tests and saved-output replay pass with unchanged decisions and final frames. That change has not had a new model run, so I am not claiming it fixes the unresolved cases.
