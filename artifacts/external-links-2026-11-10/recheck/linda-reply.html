Aleksandr,

Thanks. I agree they are complementary.

Per the DAWN charter, the discovered AI resource may itself be a service such as a catalog that furthers the discovery process. So bootstrap discovery can return the minimum public information needed to reach such a catalog.

I would still keep the metadata reference resource-specific, e.g., pointing to an Agent Card. Catalog search, capability comparison, ranking, or selection after reaching the catalog remain outside the bootstrap scope.

Linda

Hi Linda,

Thank's for the update.

The clear boundary in -03 is helpful: bootstrap discovery returns the minimum needed to reach an agent, while catalog search, ranking and cost-based selection stay out of scope.
That excluded part is what draft-artamokhov-dawn-marketplace-discovery addresses: how a requester finds catalogs (marketplaces) that list agents and tools across operators, through a well-known URI, before any selection happens.
The two seem complementary: your model finds an entry point inside a managed network, and a catalog is used when the requester has to search beyond it.

One question: should the metadata reference returned by bootstrap discovery be allowed to point to such a catalog, or do you see catalogs as entirely separate from bootstrap discovery?

Hi all,

Thanks to Adrian for the detailed comments, and to everyone for the helpful discussion on the DAWN mailing list.

We have updated draft https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-dunbar-dawn-infra-assisted-discovery/ . The revision aligns the terminology more closely with the DAWN terminology draft, in particular around MVD/MDI and the role of requester context. It also clarifies that the infrastructure-assisted model supports bootstrap discovery by contributing current resource state and requester context, without defining registration, catalog search, semantic matchmaking, ranking, or resource selection.

The updated draft also distinguishes more clearly between DAWN bootstrap discovery and subsequent retrieval of richer agent-native metadata such as an A2A Agent Card.

Comments and suggestions are very welcome.

Best regards,
Linda
